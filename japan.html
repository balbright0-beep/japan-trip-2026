ARRIVE TOKYO

Saturday, April 11
到着

Land at Haneda, settle into the Mesm, cherry blossoms at golden hour, yakitori smoke and tiny bars after dark.

1:35 PM
Land at Haneda Airport (HND) 📍
UA 131 from EWR. Confirmation: JKZYHF. Kyle 21A (window), Bradley 21C (aisle). Immigration + baggage ~45-60 min. After customs, exit into the 2F Arrival Lobby. 2:30 PM
Haneda Terminal 3 > Hamamatsucho TRANSIT 📍
From the 2F Arrival Lobby, follow overhead signs for 'Tokyo Monorail' (not Keikyu — Keikyu goes to Shinagawa, which works but is slower to Hamamatsucho). Saturday discount: look for the 'Monorail + Yamanote Discount Ticket' (540 yen) at the vending machines beside the gate — saves a few yen if you're heading onward by JR.

4:45 PM
Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden 📍
Peak cherry blossoms, extended spring hours until 6:30 PM in April. Three garden styles (Japanese, English, French) in one park. No alcohol allowed. 500 yen entry. Enter via the Shinjuku Gate (closest to the station, 10-min walk from West Exit — head south on Koshu-kaido, turn left at Shinjuku Gyoen-mae intersection).
🏮 Originally the private residence of a feudal lord, Shinjuku Gyoen became an imperial garden in 1906. It has over 1,000 cherry trees across 65 varieties — one of the longest blooming seasons in Tokyo because different varieties peak at different times.
6:30 PM
Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building 📍
Free 45F observation deck (South Tower). Sunset views over the city. No reservation needed. 10-min walk northwest from Gyoen — walk back toward Shinjuku Station, continue past the station to the twin towers visible to the west.
🏮 At 243m, the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building was the tallest building in Japan when completed in 1991. Architect Kenzo Tange designed the twin towers to resemble the Notre-Dame cathedral in Paris — visible in the split facade.
7:30 PM
Omoide Yokocho (Memory Lane) EAT 📍
From the Government Building, walk east ~8 min back toward Shinjuku Station. Omoide Yokocho is on the WEST side of the tracks, immediately north of the West Exit. Look for the narrow smoky alleyways tucked between the tracks and the main road. Try Motsu-yaki Ucchan for grilled organ meats: the Negi Liver (chicken liver with yuzu kosho) is a revelation. Dango Tsukune meatball skewers with just salt. Cash only at most stalls.
🏮 Omoide Yokocho ('Memory Lane') began as a black market after WWII, selling scraps of food to starving Tokyoites. The tiny stalls have barely changed since the 1940s. Locals also call it 'Piss Alley' (Shonben Yokocho) — a nickname that stuck.
9:00 PM
Golden Gai 📍
From Omoide Yokocho, walk EAST through Shinjuku Station (use the East Exit passageway, ~5 min) or walk around the station via Yasukuni-dori (~10 min). Golden Gai is a grid of six narrow alleys north of Yasukuni-dori, east of the Kabukicho entrance arch. 200+ micro-bars, most seating 5-10 people. Small cover (~500 yen) at most places. Ramen Nagi is on the 2F at 1-1-10 Kabukicho, right at the edge of Golden Gai: 24-hour niboshi ramen, order the Sugoi Niboshi bowl (~1,300 yen) from the ticket vending machine outside.
🏮 Golden Gai survived Japan's 1980s bubble economy when developers tried to buy the entire block for redevelopment. The bar owners collectively refused to sell, and two suspicious fires broke out before public outcry saved the district. Many bars still have 'rules' posted outside — some welcome foreigners, others are regulars-only.
11:00 PM

Platform 14 is on the south side of the JR area; follow green Yamanote signs from the East Exit or Central East Exit. LAST TRAIN: The final inner-loop departure from Platform 14 is approximately 0:15-0:33 AM (varies slightly by day). Ongoing Shinagawa-area construction has shifted some times — aim to board by midnight to be safe. Check the digital departure board at the platform for the exact last train. Fare: ~220 yen IC. Missed it? Taxi from Shinjuku to Hamamatsucho: ~3,000-4,000 yen, ~15 min late at night.

ALL OF TOKYO + NIGHT BLOSSOMS + NI-CHOME

Sunday, April 12
桜

Tsukiji for breakfast, old-town Yanaka, indie Shimokitazawa, illuminated cherry blossoms on the Meguro River, and Ni-chome after dark. Sunday is still lively in the bars.

8:00 AM
Hamamatsucho > Daimon > Tsukijishijo TRANSIT 📍
From Mesm, walk to JR Hamamatsucho (~6 min). But do NOT enter JR. Instead, look for the 'Daimon Station' entrance — it's at street level, a 1-min walk from JR Hamamatsucho's North Exit. Daimon (station code E20) is on the Toei Oedo Line. Enter via Entrance A3 (closest from Hamamatsucho). Descend to the deep underground platform. Board toward Tsukijishijo/Ryogoku direction (decreasing station numbers: E19, E18...). Ride just 2 stops to Tsukijishijo (E18), ~4 min. Fare: ~180 yen IC. At Tsukijishijo, take Exit A1 — you'll emerge right at the edge of Tsukiji Outer Market. Turn left and the market streets are immediately ahead.
8:30 AM
Tsukiji Outer Market EAT 📍
Street food breakfast — this is one of the great food experiences in Tokyo. The outer market sprawls across several narrow lanes packed with ~400 shops and stalls. Start at the Jogai Shijo entrance and work your way through. Must-tries: tamagoyaki (sweet rolled omelette on a stick, ~300 yen) from Yamazaki or Shouro, grilled scallop (hotate) with butter and soy sauce (~500 yen), fresh uni (sea urchin) on a small rice ball (~800 yen), and a tuna sashimi plate from any stall with a queue. Walk the entire market before committing to a sit-down meal. Budget ~2,000-3,000 yen for a full grazing breakfast. Peak crowd hits around 10 AM — you're arriving before the rush.
🏮 The original Tsukiji fish market operated here from 1935 to 2018, when the inner wholesale market moved to Toyosu. At its peak, Tsukiji handled over 2,000 tons of seafood daily — more than any market in the world. The outer market stayed put and is now purely for food tourists and locals.
10:00 AM
Tsukiji > Asakusa TRANSIT 📍
From the market, walk east on Harumi-dori (the broad avenue that runs past the market entrance) for ~5-7 min. You'll reach Higashi-Ginza Station (station code A11) on the Toei Asakusa Line — the station entrance is right next to the ornate facade of the Kabuki-za Theatre (you can't miss it: a massive traditional Japanese building). Descend the stairs and go to Platform 2 (northbound, toward Asakusa and Oshiage). Board any train — all types stop at Asakusa. Ride directly to Asakusa (A18): 7 stops, ~11 min, no transfer needed. Fare: ~180 yen IC. At Asakusa Station, take Exit A4 — this puts you right in front of Senso-ji's famous Kaminarimon gate (the big red lantern framed by two guardian statues). This route via the Asakusa Line is faster and simpler than the older route of walking to Shimbashi and taking the Ginza Line.
10:15 AM
Senso-ji + Asakusa 📍
Tokyo's oldest temple, founded 645 AD — over 1,300 years of continuous worship. Enter through the massive Kaminarimon ('Thunder Gate') with its iconic red lantern, then walk the 250-meter Nakamise-dori shopping street (Japan's oldest, established in the 1700s) lined with stalls selling senbei rice crackers, ningyo-yaki cakes shaped like temple bells, and traditional fans. At the main hall, waft incense smoke over yourself for good health, toss a coin, clap twice, and make a wish. Draw an omikuji fortune slip (100 yen) — if you get 'daikichi' (great blessing), celebrate; if 'kyo' (curse), tie it to the wire rack to leave the bad luck behind. The five-story pagoda to the left is photogenic. Free entry. Allow 45-60 min.
🏮 Senso-ji was founded in 645 AD after two fishermen found a golden statue of Kannon (goddess of mercy) in the Sumida River. They kept throwing it back, but it kept returning. The temple was built on the spot. The massive Kaminarimon lantern weighs 700kg and has been rebuilt several times after fires — the current one was donated by the founder of Panasonic in 1960.
11:30 AM
Asakusa > Nippori (for Yanaka) TRANSIT 📍
Walk to Tokyo Metro Asakusa Station (station code G19) — it's a separate entrance from the Toei station, ~2-min walk west toward Kaminarimon. Descend to the Ginza Line platform. Board Platform 1 (southbound toward Shibuya/Ueno). Ride 3 stops to Ueno (G16), ~5 min. Fare: ~170 yen IC. At Ueno, follow signs for 'JR Lines' — exit the Metro gate, walk ~3 min through the underground passage, and re-enter JR Ueno. Go to Platform 2 (Yamanote Line OUTER LOOP, clockwise, toward Nippori/Tabata). Ride 2 stops to Nippori, ~4 min. Fare: ~150 yen IC. At Nippori, exit via the West Exit. Walk straight ahead (west) ~3 min — you'll hit the famous Yuyake Dandan sunset stairs leading down to Yanaka Ginza shopping street.
🏮 Yanaka survived the 1923 Great Kanto Earthquake and the 1945 firebombings that destroyed most of Tokyo. The neighborhood's low wooden buildings and narrow lanes preserve an Edo-period atmosphere that's almost entirely vanished elsewhere in the city. Over 70 temples are packed into this small area — more than almost anywhere else in Tokyo.
11:45 AM
Yanaka 📍
Tokyo's best-preserved old neighborhood, largely untouched by war and redevelopment. Start at the Yuyake Dandan stairs — the view down the shotengai at sunset is famous but lovely any time. Walk through Yanaka Cemetery: a tranquil 100,000 sq meter park with cherry blossom-lined paths and graves of shoguns, samurai, and artists. Visit Tennoji Temple (one of the oldest in Tokyo) at the cemetery's south end. Explore the back lanes for traditional craft shops: Isetatsu (hand-printed chiyogami paper since 1864), Yanaka Matsuno (traditional brushes), SCAI the Bathhouse (contemporary art gallery in a converted 200-year-old bathhouse). The whole area has a village-within-a-city atmosphere that's increasingly rare in Tokyo.

The 'Bonus Track' development was built on the reclaimed land above the buried highway.

Shimokitazawa 📍
Tokyo's indie heartland. Vintage clothing (Flamingo, Stick Out), record shops (Flash Disc Ranch for Japanese pressings), small theaters, and third-wave coffee (Bear Pond Espresso, Cafe Mardi). The neighborhood was rebuilt in the 2020s with open-air micro-developments like Bonus Track and Reload — clusters of small independent shops, cafes, and restaurants designed to feel like a village market. The main shopping is immediately outside the south exit.

Shimokitazawa > Nakameguro TRANSIT 📍
800+ Somei Yoshino cherry trees line both banks of the Meguro River for nearly 4km, creating a tunnel of blossoms. Walk slowly — there are boutiques, cafes (Onibus Coffee is excellent, Blue Bottle too), and tiny restaurants tucked under the trees. In the afternoon, light filters through the canopy creating a pink-tinged glow. This is widely considered the single best cherry blossom spot in Tokyo.

KAMAKURA TRAIL + ENOSHIMA ISLAND + AKIHABARA

Monday, April 13
波

Hike the forested ridge to the Great Buddha, take the coastal Enoden to a sacred island with sea caves and ocean views, then neon-lit arcades and conveyor belt sushi back in Tokyo.

7:30 AM
Tsukiji Outer Market breakfast EAT 📍
Second morning, different stalls. Same route: Daimon (E20, Entrance A3) > Oedo Line 2 stops > Tsukijishijo (E18, Exit A1). ~4 min, ~180 yen IC.
8:45 AM
Hamamatsucho > Shinagawa > Kita-Kamakura TRANSIT 📍
At JR Hamamatsucho, go to Track 3 (Yamanote outer loop) or Track 4 (Keihin-Tohoku Line southbound) — take whichever arrives first. Ride 2 stops south to Shinagawa (~5 min). At Shinagawa, exit the Yamanote/KT platform and follow signs for 'JR Yokosuka Line'. The Yokosuka Line uses Platforms 11-12 on the east (Konan) side. Board any southbound train toward Zushi, Kurihama, or Kamakura. All local Yokosuka Line trains stop at Kita-Kamakura (station code JO07). The ride from Shinagawa is ~50 min, passing through Yokohama and Ofuna. Total fare Hamamatsucho to Kita-Kamakura: ~945 yen IC (single JR fare, no separate tickets needed). WHY SHINAGAWA: Going via Tokyo Station requires descending to B5 underground platforms (10-15 min transfer). Shinagawa's Yokosuka platform is on the same level — much easier with morning energy. At Kita-Kamakura, exit via the single small ticket gate. Engakuji Temple is immediately visible across the road to the right.
🏮 Kamakura was Japan's military capital from 1185 to 1333 under the Kamakura Shogunate — the first samurai government. The surrounding mountains made it naturally defensible, and the narrow valleys (called 'kiridoshi') were carved through rock as military access routes. You'll walk through the remnants of this medieval defense network on the Daibutsu trail.
9:45 AM
Jochiji Temple (Trail Start) HIKE START 📍
From Kita-Kamakura Station, walk south along the main road ~8 min. Jochiji is on the left. 200 yen entry. Trail entrance is behind the temple — follow signs for 'Daibutsu Hiking Course'.

🌧 RAIN PLAN: If it's raining heavily, the Daibutsu Trail can be slippery and muddy. Check Kamakura city website the morning of. Backup: Skip the trail and take the Enoden directly from Kita-Kamakura to Hase Station (for the Great Buddha and Hasedera), then continue to Enoshima. The temples and island are fine in rain — actually atmospheric. Bring your rain shell.
10:15 AM
Kuzuharaoka Shrine 📍
Throw a ceramic plate at the sacred rock to ward off evil (100 yen).
10:45 AM
Zeniarai Benten Shrine 📍
Wash your coins in the sacred spring for financial fortune. Enter through a tunnel carved into rock.
11:30 AM
Great Buddha (Kotoku-in) 📍
13-meter bronze Buddha, outdoors since a tsunami destroyed its hall in the 1400s. 300 yen. Cash only. You can go inside the statue for an extra 50 yen.
🏮 The Great Buddha (Daibutsu) originally sat inside a massive wooden hall, but a tsunami in 1498 washed the building away. The bronze statue — cast in 1252 from over 121 tons of bronze — has sat in the open air for over 500 years. It once had gold leaf covering its entire surface; traces of the original gold are still visible near the right ear.
12:00 PM
Hasedera Temple 📍
Clifftop Pacific Ocean views, cave tunnel with Buddhist carvings. 7-min walk from the Buddha — head south on the main road. 400 yen.
🏮 Hasedera Temple houses a 9.18-meter golden Eleven-Headed Kannon — the largest wooden sculpture in Japan. Legend says it was carved from the same camphor tree as the Kannon at Hasedera in Nara, and was set adrift in Osaka Bay in 721 AD. It washed ashore at Kamakura 15 years later.
1:00 PM
Komachi-dori Street lunch EAT 📍
From Hasedera, walk to Hase Station on the Enoden (~5 min south). Take the Enoden 3 stops east to Kamakura Station (~8 min, ~200 yen). Exit and walk north on Komachi-dori (~2 min from the east exit). Shirasu (whitebait) is Kamakura's signature: try shirasu-don (whitebait rice bowl) at any of the restaurants along the street. ~800-1,200 yen.
🏮 Komachi-dori is named after Ono no Komachi, a legendary 9th-century poet famous for her beauty. She's one of the 'Six Immortals of Poetry' (Rokkasen). The street has been Kamakura's main shopping street since the Meiji era (1868+), though the current tourist-friendly version dates from the 1970s.
2:00 PM
Kamakura Station > Enoshima TRANSIT 📍
The Enoden (Enoshima Electric Railway) station is attached to JR Kamakura Station on its WEST side. From the JR gates, follow signs for 'Enoden' — there's a JR-Enoden transfer gate that lets you switch seamlessly with your Suica (no need to exit and re-enter). The Enoden has ONE platform at Kamakura — it's the eastern terminus, so ALL trains go toward Fujisawa/Enoshima. You cannot board the wrong direction. Ride 9 stops to Enoshima Station (EN06), ~25 min. Fare: 260 yen IC. Suica confirmed accepted on Enoden. Trains depart every ~12 min. The ride is scenic — ocean views between Inamuragasaki (EN08) and Kamakura-Koko-Mae, plus the famous Slam Dunk railway crossing. At Enoshima Station (a charming dragon-castle-themed building), exit and walk south ~10 min across the Benten Bridge to the island.
🏮 According to legend, Enoshima Island rose from the sea in 552 AD when the goddess Benzaiten descended from heaven to stop a five-headed dragon that had been terrorizing the area. She married the dragon and pacified him — the three-scale 'Mitsuuroko' crest you'll see throughout the island represents the dragon's scales.
2:30 PM
Cross Benten Bridge to Enoshima Island 📍

Packing List

CLOTHING

April in Tokyo/Kyoto ranges 12–20°C (54–68°F). Mornings and evenings are cool; midday can feel warm in the sun. The key is layering — you'll peel off and add back throughout the day.

  • T-shirts / base layers ×4Lightweight, breathable, quick-dry. Merino wool or synthetic blends work best — cotton stays damp from sweat on hiking days. You'll do laundry zero times, so pack one per two days plus a spare.
  • Long-sleeve midlayer ×2A flannel, light sweater, or quarter-zip. This is your main warmth layer for cool mornings (Fushimi Inari dawn at ~8°C, Government Building sunset, evening canal walks). Bring two so you can alternate.
  • Light jacket or hoodieSomething warm enough for the Mt. Takao summit, the Fuji Excursion train (Kawaguchiko is ~5°C cooler than Tokyo at 831m elevation), and late nights in Shinjuku. Not so bulky that it's annoying to carry in your daypack midday.
  • Packable rain shellNon-negotiable. April is the start of spring rain season. A compact waterproof jacket that stuffs into its own pocket is essential — you'll carry it daily. No umbrellas on the four hiking days (Kamakura trail, Mt. Takao, Chureito stairs, Fushimi Inari).
  • Pants: 1 stretch/hikingThese will be your workhorse for hiking days (3, 4, 5, 7) and heavy walking days. Stretchy, quick-dry, comfortable for 20,000+ steps. Avoid jeans on hike days — they restrict movement and are miserable when wet.
  • Pants: 1 casualFor city days, Ni-chome nightlife, and Roan Kikunoi dinner. Dark chinos or clean joggers work. Shorts are fine for casual walking but not appropriate inside temples, shrines, or kaiseki restaurants.
  • Underwear ×7One week's worth. If you run short, Uniqlo AIRism underwear (~¥990/pack) is everywhere and genuinely excellent. Every major station has a Uniqlo within 5 min.
  • Socks ×7 (incl. 2 hiking)Bring at least 2 pairs of proper hiking socks (merino or padded synthetic) for trail days. Regular socks for city days. You'll remove shoes constantly at temples, restaurants, and the onsen — no holes.
  • Nice outfit for KikunoiSmart casual — collared shirt or clean sweater, your casual pants, clean shoes. No dress code at Roan Kikunoi but you'll feel out of place in hiking gear at a 2-star Michelin kaiseki. Also works for Isoyama (Pontocho, Day 6).
